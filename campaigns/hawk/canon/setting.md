# H.A.W.K Setting

Pitch, conflicts, history, tone, magic as technology, travel and scale.

## Pitch

H.A.W.K stands for **Hunts, Acquisitions, Whispers and Knowledge**. It is an organization that manages adventuring across the world, matching adventurers with clients for missions and tasks. It used to be a true adventurers' guild; now it offers what it calls "Questing as a Service", to streamline adventuring and run it as a professional business. H.A.W.K is not a major political power, but its control of adventuring resources and information makes it a key player in the world's events.

The setting is a world where adventuring is a professional industry and the balance of power between factions is always shifting. The War in Heaven adds high-stakes conflict, with mortal and divine forces both driving events. Magic is technology: it is built into industry, war, travel and daily life, and shapes the world's infrastructure and society.

Touchstones: Eberron, Mistborn, Stormlight Archive and other high-fantasy settings where magic is part of society and industry. There is a cyberpunk thread too: advanced technology and magic blend into everyday life, and information, power and resources are always contested.

## Main Conflicts

The big tensions set the gods against the mortals, and the mortals against each other. The gods are parasitic entities that feed on mortal belief and power, and they seek to keep and grow their hold over the world. The mortal factions (human, elven, dwarven and others, H.A.W.K among them) compete for control, influence and survival in a world shaped by both divine and mundane forces.

The War in Heaven is fought in three places:

- **The Astral Sea, in the Core Sector** — the central hub, where many of the major mortal factions and divine powers meet and compete for influence
- **The Divine Realms** — the gods' own sectors
- **The Material Plane**

## History

### The Deep Past

- **The Era of Gods** — the Material is chaos, and eldritch gods fight over it.
- **The First Era** — the Material calms, mortal civilization begins, and the Pantheons form. Mortals worship the Fey Royals.
- **The Divine Era** — the Aetheric Gods take over from the Fey Royals, and the Divine Era begins. Theocratic empires fight the gods' proxy wars and destroy themselves. The surviving gods form the Celestial and Infernal Courts.
- **c. 770 ED** — Morgana La Fey, the First Witch, is born, and settlers found Witchlyn. She is also known as Baba Yaga.
- **795 ED** — mortals start working magic without the gods, and the gods hunt the early covens. The Thymian Court of elven archmagi rises into the Astral Sea and builds The Shard.
- **The end of the Divine Era** — the Pact is formed, binding the Fey Royals. It was never meant to allow arcane magic; that it opened the way to the Era of Magic was a happy accident.

### The Era of Magic

- Arcane magic takes over and Arcano-Tech brings a modern age. People see the gods as obsolete, except the Thymian nations, which worship their archmagi as gods.
- **17 EA** — the First Witch, under the name Baba Yaga, is exiled from the Thymian Court. She works on the Weave throughout her exile.
- **140 EA to about 440 EA** — the Great War, or Era of Strife, forms today's nations.
- **345 EA** — Ulsidor the Mage founds his Laboratory on "the Mountain", and its technology becomes the base of modern society.
- **400 EA** — the Trade Cities and the Alliance of Open Seas form. H.A.W.K is founded in Witchlyn as a guild for adventurers left over from the war.
- **The Year of Ash** (410 or 455 EA; the old record disagrees with itself) — planar energy mutates the Huron region, and the Republic of Urn is founded.
- **505 EA** — Ulsidor the Mad Mage reportedly dies. He is in fact still alive.

### The Last Fifty Years

- **655, the Year of Dark** — the Thymian Court eclipses the moon, and arcane magic stops working. This is season 1, *The Fall of Magic*.
- **655, the 5-Day War** (23–28 Peony) — the Rebellion beats the Thymian Inquisition in Witchlyn, Iskalur and Porta Sol. It lasted six days; nobody has corrected the name.
- **29 Peony 655, Diem Veritas** — every being learns the truth about the gods. It is now a holiday.
- **655** — Morgana releases the Weave to the Material Plane, freeing it from needing the gods.
- **655** — Veil appears above Witchlyn with Morgana, as a bridge to the Astral Sea.
- **660** — the Dragonkin colony in the Thymian ruins breaks away from the Queendom of Dragons, and dragons settle the Divine Coast.
- **662** — United Aduain disbands itself. Power spreads out, and private businesses and factions fill the gap.
- **665** — Veil partners with Witchlyn, H.A.W.K and others, and offers open citizenship.
- **672** — O.W.L. and the Coven found Haven, the first Material Plane colony in the Astral Sea.
- **680** — Blackwater Industries arrives at Veil and offers a trade deal, with The Laboratory as liaison.
- **695** — forces gather at the borders of the Astral Realms. What remains of the Celestial and Infernal Courts skirmishes, and armadas fly the dead Thymian Court's flag.
- **705** — H.A.W.K sanctions its first Phoenix-Class Adventurers: the party.
- **Around 707** — season 3 opens. Blackwater Industries has fallen as a megacorporation, and the Core Sector is neutral ground.

## Tone

If something can be both funny and horrifying, it should be. The setting thrives on the tension between absurdity and darkness, and the best stories come when both are fully embraced. The party should feel like characters from The Office or Parks and Rec, dropped into a very serious movie.

- **Dual register** — corporate satire, absurdist humor and irreverent naming sit over genuinely dark themes such as indoctrination, labor exploitation and demonic corruption. Neither one is flattened; the joke and the horror can be the same object.
- **Naming** — tongue-in-cheek aliases, absurdist acronyms (UwU, O.W.L.) and puns grounded in the setting, matched to the register of whatever is named. Corporate and alchemical terms carry a second, sinister meaning.
- **Less is more** — for names and flavor text, the best few options, not a long list.
- **In bounds** — torture in detail, graphic body horror, and real-world religions reflected in the gods.
- **Out of bounds** — sexual violence; harm to children shown in detail; real-world slurs and hate groups; self-harm and suicide; slavery played for laughs.

## Magic as Technology

Magic powers everything: industry, war, travel and daily life.

There are two main sources of magical power:

- **Raw Aether** — the fundamental magical energy that fills the Astral Sea. It must be processed and concentrated into stable forms before industry, war, travel and daily life can use it. Aetherstones are the small form; Aether-Cores are the large one.
- **The Weave** — the structured network of magical currents that allows controlled spellcasting and the shaping of magical effects. It is more reliable than raw Aether but less abundant. It exists only on the Material Plane, and only mortals control it. O.W.L. and other mortal factions are working to extend it beyond the Material Plane.

Access varies from place to place. Under Blackwater, Aether-tech was available across much of the Core Sector. During the War in Heaven, access is restricted and uneven, and different factions control key sources and technologies. The Weave stays more stable and accessible where mortal control is strong, and varies with local magical infrastructure and the presence of skilled spellcasters.

## Travel

- **On the Material Plane** — many forms of Arcano-Tech travel, from the Lightning Rail to arcane airships to teleportation gates. Speed, cost and magical infrastructure decide how accessible and how safe each one is.
- **Between the planes** — Veil is the gateway between the Material Plane and the Astral Sea.
- **On the Astral Sea** — only Aether-powered vessels travel here, riding the currents of the Drift: the space between realms, full of unpredictable magical phenomena and dangerous entities. The navigator's skill and the quality of the vessel's Aether engines decide whether a passage is safe. On the Core Sector grid, the clouds show the Drift.
- **Between sectors** — Aetherships cross from the Core Sector into the rim sectors through gates. The charted gates are Solar Gate, Axial Gate, Tranquil Gate and Abaddon's Gate.

> **GM-only:** the Abyss and the Perilous Wilds have gates too. Almost no one knows they exist.

## Scale

For now, the faction game maps only the Core Sector. The full game will have a map for each realm. The number of places and factions that matter changes over time; the focus is on the major powers and the key places that move the balance of power.

Each sector is a large, distinct region of the Astral Sea, with its own character, resources and power dynamics. The Core Sector is the most developed and the most fiercely contested. It is neutral ground, and Blackwater used to control it. The rim sectors are more sparsely populated and are often held by other factions or independent powers. Each rim sector is the realm of an Astral God: a Divine Realm, aligned toward one end of one of the three axes. It shapes the balance of power and the flow of magical energy within its borders.

| Sector | Trait | Axis | Ruler |
|--------|-------|------|-------|
| Solar | Light | Light and Dark | Sol |
| Abaddon | Dark | Light and Dark | Asmodeus |
| Axiom | Order | Order and Chaos | Nyadra'zatha |
| Abyss | Chaos | Order and Chaos | Lilith |
| Perilous Wilds | Activity | Activity and Passivity | Unknown |
| Tranquility | Passivity | Activity and Passivity | Unknown |

On the sector map, opposite ends face each other across the Core Sector:

```
                 Solar
   Tranquility           Axiom
                 Core
   Abyss                 Perilous Wilds
                Abaddon
```

A Fragment holds Aether from only a few traits. The Material Plane is the same kind of thing as a Fragment, but it is the one perfect nexus of all six traits.

## The Gods

After season 1, the old divine courts broke apart. The Celestial Court split into three bodies: the Radiant Throne, the Justicar and the Sisters of Silence. The Court of Lies is what remains of the Infernal Court.

### The Radiant Throne

- **Sol** — The Light Bringer. Rules Solar.
- **Rae** — Daughter of the Sun. Sol's daughter.

### The Justicar

- **Fenrir** — The Judge
- **Bahamut** — The Dragon King

### The Sisters of Silence

- **Nocturna** — Daughter of the Moon
- **Harlequin** — Lady Luck

### The Court of Lies

- **Asmodeus** — Lord of Abaddon. Rules Abaddon.
- **Tenebra** — The Mother of Dragons. Serves Asmodeus.
- **Lilith** — The Betrayer. Rules the Abyss.

### The Fey Royals

- **Ophelia** — The Fungal Queen
- **Gwyndolin** — The Crimson Divinity
- **Sardior** — The Lady Valentine
- **Oberon** — The Dawn King
- **Nyx** — The Queen of Night
- **Anathema** — Harbinger of the Void
- **Tatania** — The Breath of the Wild
- **Luna** — daughter of Oberon and Tatania

### Astral Gods

- **Nyadra'zatha** — The Mother Arcane. Rules Axiom. An Astral God in her own right, and an ally of mortalkind. She was imprisoned for a very long time.

### The Unknown Gods

The gods of Tranquility and the Perilous Wilds are unknown.

> **GM-only:** not decided yet. They may have been consumed by other gods, or may simply be unknown.
